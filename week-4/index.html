01 / Context

A routine should not feel like another task.

Busy people may abandon tracking when it requires too much input or presents discouraging feedback. Stride explores a smaller interaction: choose an activity, add minutes, review and save.

My role

UX research synthesis, information architecture, visual system, copy, prototype and developer handoff.

Research boundary

Desk research only. Personas and journey emotions are hypotheses. No interviews, user tests or measured business outcomes are claimed.

02 / Research

Less effort. More control.

Published qualitative research describes the value of clear guidance and self-monitoring, alongside cognitive burden, reminder preferences and privacy concerns. I translated these directions into minimal inputs, optional reminders and supportive weekly feedback.

Health-app engagement study (2021) · Health behavior change study (2013)

Competitive analysis

Strava emphasizes activity tracking, sharing and training tools. MyFitnessPal’s Today tab combines meals, water, exercise and weight logging. Stride proposes a narrower activity-first scope; this is a positioning hypothesis, not evidence of a market gap.

What still needs evidence

Interview 6–8 target users; test five participants on setting a goal, logging a walk, editing and finding weekly totals. Proposed targets: 4/5 unaided completion and logging under 60 seconds. These are targets, not results.

The prototype uses in-memory demo data and sends no notifications. Browser usability and accessibility checks remain necessary; no full WCAG conformance is claimed.
